The Driving Point for Oscillation and the Instantaneous Center of Rotation in a Uniform Wooden Rod
IB Extended Essay, Physics HL (Rigid Body Dynamics), 2026

Summary

Inspired by the "sweet spot" of a baseball bat, this essay asks how the position of the driving point for oscillation (xosc) determines the position of the instantaneous center of rotation (xICR) in a 100 cm uniform pine rod. Treating small-amplitude oscillation as a rapid succession of impacts, I derived xICR = −(L2/12)·xosc−1 from the superposition of translational and rotational motion. I built a wooden support frame and an intermediate linkage driven by a linear actuator (2.6–3.4 Hz), filmed nine driving points at 60 fps, and tracked 19 holes along the rod in Tracker. Fourier spectra isolated the driving frequency, sinusoidal regression gave each hole's amplitude, and the zero-amplitude intercept located the ICR. The data follow xICR = (−924 ± 198)·xosc−1.08 ± 0.09 (R2 ≈ 0.998), consistent with the predicted inverse law (exponent −1, constant −833), with the pivot always on the opposite side of the center of mass. The same principle lets a robotic arm strike or capture objects at its center of percussion to minimize shock transmitted to its joints and base.

Phase Portraits of a One-Degree-of-Freedom Quarter-Car Suspension
IB Mathematics: Applications and Interpretation HL Internal Assessment, 2026

Summary

Motivated by how differently a car and a bus ride over the same speed bump, this exploration models one wheel of a car as a 1-DOF quarter-car (m = 400 kg, k = 10 000 N m−1) released 0.1 m above equilibrium. I rewrote mẍ + cẋ + kx = 0 as a coupled first-order system X′ = MX and used its characteristic equation λ2 + (c/400)λ + 25 = 0 to show that the suspension is stable for every c > 0 and is critically damped at c = 4000 N s m−1. Solving exactly with eigenvalues and eigenvectors for critically damped (λ = −5, repeated), over-damped (λ = −2.5, −10) and under-damped (ζ = 0.3, λ ≈ −1.5 ± 4.77i) suspensions, I drew their phase portraits in Python. They show a degenerate node, a node whose final approach follows the slow eigenvector v = −2.5x, and a clockwise spiral that keeps only about 14% of its amplitude after each oscillation, which is the repeated bouncing I felt on the bus. Which damping is "best" depends on whether settling time, overshoot or comfort is being optimized.

Volumetric Flow Rate of Water and Pipe Length and Radius under Constant Hydrostatic Pressure
IB Physics HL Internal Assessment, 2026

Summary

Noticing that different café straws deliver very different amounts of drink, I measured how the volumetric flow rate Q of water depends on the radius (1–3 mm) and length (10–50 cm) of acrylic pipes. A custom-built, continuously overflowing acrylic tank held the water head, and therefore the driving pressure, constant, and Q was found by weighing the water discharged in 30 s. Combining the two power laws gave Q = 10.71·r2.3 ± 0.8/L0.38 ± 0.05 (R2 = 0.997 for the combined fit). Compared with a turbulent model derived from the Darcy–Weisbach equation and the Blasius correlation (Q ∝ r2.71L−0.571), the radius exponent agreed within uncertainty but the length exponent differed significantly. Calculating the Reynolds numbers (about 1200–6500) explained why: the flows spanned laminar, transitional and turbulent regimes, so no single-regime model applies, and I proposed how to redesign the apparatus to keep every pipe in one regime.

The Effect of Temperature on the Equilibrium Constant of Iron(III) Thiocyanate
IB Chemistry HL Internal Assessment, 2026

Summary

Using a UV-Vis spectrophotometer and a Beer–Lambert calibration curve, I measured how temperature (10–50 °C) affects the equilibrium constant of iron(III) thiocyanate. Kc fell from 310 ± 50 to 200 ± 30, as Le Chatelier's principle predicts for an exothermic reaction, and a van 't Hoff plot was linear (R2 = 0.97). Values about twice the literature value revealed a systematic error, which I traced mainly to the lower ionic strength of my solutions.

Egg Shortages and the Opportunity Cost of Choosing Free-Range Eggs in Australia
IB Economics SL Internal Assessment, Commentary 1 (Microeconomics), 2025
source article (The Guardian)

Summary

Using supply and demand diagrams, this commentary analyzes Australia's shift from battery-cage to free-range eggs. Because both demand and supply are price-inelastic, an avian flu outbreak that shifts free-range supply only slightly causes a large price rise. I argue that the opportunity cost of choosing free-range eggs is a cheaper, more reliable supply, and that barn-laid eggs and stronger biosecurity can reduce it.

Real Wages, Inflation and the Reserve Bank of Australia's Rate Rises
IB Economics SL Internal Assessment, Commentary 2 (Macroeconomics), 2026
source article (ABC News)

Summary

Australian wages rose 3.4% while prices rose 3.8%, so real wages fell by about 0.4%. Using AD/AS diagrams, this commentary models a mix of demand-pull and cost-push inflation and the RBA's contractionary monetary policy. I argue that higher interest rates hurt workers and mortgage holders in the short run, especially in sectors with low wage growth, but are needed to prevent stagflation and let real wages recover.

China's Anti-Dumping Tariffs on EU Pork and Cross-Sector Interdependence
IB Economics SL Internal Assessment, Commentary 3 (Global Economy), 2026
source article (Reuters)

Summary

Using tariff diagrams, this commentary compares China's preliminary (up to 62.4%) and final (mostly 9.8%) anti-dumping duties on EU pork. The lower final rate shrinks producer gains and deadweight loss and lets imports recover. I argue that the tariff moved more with the separate EU–China dispute over electric-vehicle tariffs than with the pork market itself: interdependence can settle two disputes at once, but it passes risk onto exporters.
